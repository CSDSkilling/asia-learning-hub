# Past Investigation: Oven Heating Conditions

Synthetic Contoso training record. Fictional incident year: 2025. Location: Plant A, Line 1, Power Module.

## Summary

A new soldering material batch was introduced without revalidating the oven's temperature and time conditions. The first-inspection pass rate fell. This closed historical case provides a precedent for investigation, not proof of another incident's cause.

## Symptom

Inspectors found weak soldered connections after the material change. Higher oven readings coincided with more first-inspection failures.

## Investigation

- Engineers checked temperature-sensor accuracy and measured the full temperature and time curve, not only a shift-average reading.
- Zone 3 readings exceeded the approved range. The existing heating conditions had not been qualified for the new batch.
- Controlled trials compared old and new material batches under matched conditions. The new batch showed greater sensitivity to the unsuitable heating conditions.
- After engineers qualified revised heating conditions, the pass rate recovered while the material batch and operators remained unchanged.

## Root cause

Material change control omitted revalidation of the oven heating conditions, also called the reflow profile. The unsuitable conditions and the new batch's sensitivity jointly produced the defects in this historical incident.

## Corrective action

Engineering approved and qualified revised heating conditions, verified the temperature sensor, and required a documented process review for future material changes.

## Lessons

Check temperature trends and material changes together. A batch change alone does not establish cause. A shift-average temperature cannot replace a measured heating curve or defect inspection. Similarity to this case should guide controlled validation; the Quality Engineer must judge the evidence for each new incident.
