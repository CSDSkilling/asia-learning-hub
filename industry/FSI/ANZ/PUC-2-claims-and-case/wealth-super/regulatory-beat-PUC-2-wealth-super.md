# PUC-2 · Wealth & Superannuation — regulatory beat

Delivered at Exercise 7. This variant carries the highest vulnerability exposure in the library —
claimants are unwell, bereaved, or in financial distress. Deliver it accordingly.

## The framing (Exercise 7)

### Australia

"Two frameworks sit over what we've just done. The trustee has obligations to act in members' best
financial interests, and since **CPS 230** those obligations cover operational risk, critical
operations and material service providers. Claims and complaints handling are, for most funds,
critical operations — and if your administration or insurance is provided by another organisation,
the material service provider thread is the live one.

**CPS 234** covers information security, including classification of information assets and
notification of material incidents. These case files hold medical information, relationship
evidence, and financial hardship detail. Classification before grounding is not a formality.

I'm not going to tell you this is compliant — that's your control owner's call. I'll show you where
the platform evidence lives and what your assessment will ask."

### New Zealand

"For New Zealand entities, the **Privacy Act 2020** governs member personal information, with
**Information Privacy Principle 12** applying to disclosure outside New Zealand. Health information
in a claim file warrants particular care. **RBNZ cyber resilience guidance** puts governance at
board and senior-management level."

## Answering the common questions

### "Could this decide a TPD claim?"

**Answer first — no, and the boundary sits outside the fund in most cases.** TPD assessment is
usually made by the insurer against policy definitions, on medical evidence, by people qualified to
weigh it. The fund's role is typically to administer, advocate and communicate.

What the technology genuinely does: establish where the claim actually is, assemble what has been
provided, identify what is outstanding and who holds it, retrieve the governing definitions, and
draft member communications for review. Given that these cases routinely span three organisations,
"where is this claim actually up to" is a genuinely hard question today — and answering it faster is
worth a great deal to a member who is unwell.

### "What about death benefit cases — the trustee has to decide who gets it."

**Answer first.** Yes, and that decision stays exactly where it is. Determining who receives a death
benefit can involve a trustee decision and a right of objection, which is precisely why these cases
run long and why the process is deliberate.

Assembling evidence, tracking what has been provided, identifying gaps and drafting communications
are all supportable. The determination is not. And this is the case type where a communication that
implies an outcome does the most harm — a family told the wrong thing during bereavement is a
failure no efficiency gain offsets.

### "These members are vulnerable. Should we be using AI here at all?"

**Answer first — and take this question seriously, because it is a good one.** The strongest
argument for using it here is the member's experience, not the operational saving. Today a TPD
claimant often waits without knowing where their claim is, is asked repeatedly for documents they
have already sent, and receives updates that say nothing. Fixing that is a conduct improvement, not
just an efficiency one.

What must not happen: generated communications going out unreviewed, tone that implies an outcome,
or a process that feels more automated to someone who needs a person. Run Exercise 3's "more
reassuringly" counter-example on a hardship or bereavement scenario and let the room feel the
difference.

The honest framing: use it to prepare the human, never to replace them in the conversation.

### "Our administrator and insurer hold most of the data. Does this apply to us?"

**Answer first — yes, and more so.** Outsourcing the function does not outsource the obligation.
Under CPS 230 the trustee remains responsible for its critical operations and for managing risks
arising from reliance on material service providers, including the contractual terms that
relationship requires.

Practically: if you cannot establish where a member's claim is, that is a member outcome problem
regardless of whose system holds the answer.

## Where to point people

### Prudential and trustee obligations

- [APRA CPS 230: Operational Risk Management](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230) — applies
  to superannuation trustees as well as banks and insurers.
- [APRA CPS 234: Information Security](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-234)
- [Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) — Microsoft compliance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/compliance/regulatory/offering-apra-australia)

### Conduct and complaints

- [ASIC](https://asic.gov.au/) — conduct regulator for disclosure and complaints handling.
- [Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA)](https://www.afca.org.au/) — external dispute
  resolution, including superannuation complaints.

### New Zealand

- [NZ Privacy Commissioner: Principle 12, Disclosure Outside New Zealand](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/12/)
- [RBNZ: Cyber Resilience for Regulated Entities](https://www.rbnz.govt.nz/regulation-and-supervision/cross-sector-oversight/improving-cyber-resilience-for-regular-entities)
- [Financial Advice Provider (FAP) — Financial Markets Authority](https://www.fma.govt.nz/business/services/financial-advice-provider/)

### Microsoft data, privacy and governance

- [Data, Privacy, and Security for Microsoft Copilot](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/microsoft-365-copilot-privacy)
- [Microsoft 365 data residency — overview and definitions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/enterprise/m365-dr-overview)
- [Microsoft Purview protections for generative AI apps](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/ai-microsoft-purview)
- [Microsoft Service Trust Portal](https://servicetrust.microsoft.com/)
