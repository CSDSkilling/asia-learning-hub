## Trainer notes — what is deliberately in this file

Do not read this section to the room. Use it to steer.

- **The contradiction.** On 15 April the assessor records the building quote as outstanding, but the
  customer states on 21 April they sent a quote on 2 April, and the file shows only photos received
  that day. Exercise 1's prompt asks for inconsistencies between documents — this is the one.
- **The unanswered question.** The customer asked about temporary accommodation on 2 April and again
  on 14 May. Neither note records an answer, and the policy schedule shows it is included. This is
  the customer-effort moment, and it usually lands harder with a room than the money does.
- **The real gap.** The make-safe vendor noted corrosion, and the policy excludes rust, corrosion
  and gradual deterioration. The assessor flagged a possible specialist roof report but never
  requested one. Exercise 2 should surface this — it is the single item blocking the file.
- **The partially legible contents list.** A genuine evidence-quality problem, and a good prompt for
  discussing what AI should and should not infer from poor-quality source material.
- **Two reassignments in 61 days.** The handover cost this file is the argument for Exercise 1.
- **The boundary.** Nothing in this file determines whether the damage is storm or maintenance. That
  is an assessor's judgment on evidence that does not yet exist. If anyone in the room — or any
  model output — reaches a coverage conclusion from this file, stop and make that the discussion.
