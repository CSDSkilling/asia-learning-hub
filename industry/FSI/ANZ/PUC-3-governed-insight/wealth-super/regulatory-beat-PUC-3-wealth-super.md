# PUC-3 · Wealth & Superannuation — regulatory beat

Delivered at Exercise 6. Answer questions properly — deflecting costs you the room.

---

## The framing

### Australia

"Two things sit over what we've just built. The trustee has obligations to act in members' best
financial interests, and since **CPS 230** those obligations extend to operational risk, critical
operations and material service providers. Member reporting and member servicing are, for most
funds, critical operations. **CPS 234** covers information security — classification of information
assets, control effectiveness, incident response, and notification of material incidents.

If your member administration is outsourced — and for many funds it is — the material service
provider thread is the live one. The data we just analysed may not originate inside your
organisation at all, which makes lineage and assurance a contractual question as well as a
technical one.

I'm not going to tell you this is compliant; that's your control owner's call. What I'll show you is
where the platform evidence lives and what questions that assessment will ask."

### New Zealand

"For New Zealand entities, the **Privacy Act 2020** governs member personal information, and
**Information Privacy Principle 12** applies to disclosure outside New Zealand. Aggregated portfolio
reporting is lower-temperature, but the moment the model joins back to member-level records it is
personal information again. **RBNZ cyber resilience guidance** puts governance of this at board and
senior-management level."

## Answering the common questions

### "Our administrator holds the data. Does any of this apply to us?"

**Answer first — yes, and more so.** Outsourcing the function does not outsource the obligation.
Under CPS 230 the trustee remains responsible for its critical operations and for managing risks
arising from reliance on material service providers, including the minimum contractual terms that
relationship requires.

Practically, this makes Exercise 5 more valuable rather than less. If you cannot demonstrate
lineage, agreed definitions and ownership for a number you are reporting to the trustee, that is a
finding waiting to happen — regardless of who runs the system it came from.

### "Can this data be used to decide which members to contact?"

**Answer first — yes, and that is a legitimate service decision.** Identifying members who would
benefit from contact is a servicing prioritisation, not advice. The data in this exercise shows
exactly that: members with the most at stake are not the most contacted.

The boundary is in what happens *next*. The moment that contact becomes a recommendation tailored to
a member's circumstances, a different regime applies — licensing, competence, suitability and
disclosure. That is the subject of PUC-1, and if this room is going to act on what they just found,
point them there deliberately.

Watch for the jump from "this segment is disengaged" to "so let's sell them something". Name it when
it happens.

### "Could an agent produce the trustee board report?"

**Answer first.** It can draft it, from governed data, with lineage — and that is genuinely useful,
because the delay between month end and a trusted number is a real constraint for most funds.

What it does not do is take responsibility for the number. A trustee board paper is a document
someone signs. The realistic pattern is drafting and assembly with named human review and approval,
and an audit trail showing who reviewed what. Build that into the agent's instructions the way
Exercise 7 does.

### "Is this compliant with APRA?"

**Answer first.** Compliance is shared. Microsoft holds independent certifications and audit reports
covering its cloud services and publishes regulatory compliance documentation — including material
responding to APRA's information paper on cloud and to CPS 234 — through the Service Trust Portal
and the compliance offerings index. APRA's CPS 230 material explicitly contemplates banks, insurers
and superannuation trustees.

But APRA regulates the trustee, not the supplier. Classifying information assets, testing controls
and evidencing that they operate stays with the fund. Say where the evidence lives; never say a
product "is compliant".
